# Storyline and slide-deck outline (midway presentation, RQ1: Pipeline A vs Pipeline B)

*Written for: Kareem, and the Claude Code session helping build the slide deck. About 16 main slides for roughly 12 minutes, plus backup slides. Every number comes from `03_numbers_registry.md`; every figure exists in `pipeline_outputs/figures/` (generated by `scripts/make_slide_figures.py`).*

## The one-sentence story
We asked whether routing LLM judgments through a signed network (B) tracks real polarization better than averaging them as text scores (A). Yasmina built the data and two LLM-free yardsticks; I built a fair-fight pipeline and froze the model without touching the yardsticks; on a 22% prefix of our 5% sample, neither pipeline tracks the truth, B passes our pre-registered rule narrowly only because A runs the wrong way, and we know exactly what to do next.

## Act 1. The question (slides 1 to 2)

**Slide 1: Title and question.** "Is the Network Worth Building? LLM scores vs LLM-signed networks for measuring polarization." RQ1 in one line.
- Say: "Two ways to measure polarization from conversations. A: score the text. B: turn the same judgments into a signed network and measure its structure. B is much more work. Is it worth it?"

**Slide 2: The fair-fight design.** Diagram: reply edge -> ONE LLM call -> {Pipeline A: average score | Pipeline B: signed graph -> frustration}. Two LLM-free referees on the right (GT#1, GT#2).
- Key point: both pipelines read the same cached extraction (D-S1), so any difference is structure, not prompts.
- Say the cost honestly: "so we are not an exact replication of either source paper."
- Handoff: "To grade them we need data and referees that do not use an LLM. That is Yasmina's part."

## Act 2. Data and ground truth (slides 3 to 5), Yasmina's work

**Slide 3: The data.** Reddit Politosphere, all of 2016: 42.4M comments, 855k users, 28.1M reply edges, 53 weekly windows. Cleaning: bots and deleted authors out, heavy posters out, k >= 3 activity. Whole-thread sampling at 1%, 5%, 20% so conversation structure survives. Focus on the shared venue (r/politics + r/PoliticalDiscussion), where the truth is measured (1% = 152k edges, 5% = 618k, 20% = 2.7M).
- Note: samples are independent draws, not nested (D-S5).

**Slide 4: Camps and referees.** Camp labels: 100,108 users (35,244 Dem, 64,864 Rep), about 80% agreement with self-declared votes; labels are NEVER inputs to either pipeline, only for grading.
- GT#1 "do the camps hang out in different threads?" (segregation index minus shuffle null; mean 0.174). Leans toward B.
- GT#2 "can language tell the camps apart?" (phrase model transferred to neutral subs; mean 0.514 vs null 0.500; AUC about 0.70). Leans toward A.
- Decision rule: a pipeline only wins convincingly if it beats the other on the truth that leans against it (B on GT#2).

**Slide 5: What to know about the referees (honest caveats).** GT#1 is mostly composition (about 79% explained), does not track campaign events, and drops around 2016-06-27. GT#1 and GT#2 correlate 0.80 but share a spring-to-summer trend. 1% samples are weak (full-vs-1% rho 0.38), 5% is the first credible rate (0.72).
- Handoff: "That gives us a sample, labels for grading, and two yardsticks. Next: the algorithms."

## Act 3. The algorithms (slides 6 to 9), Kareem's work

**Slide 6: One LLM call per edge, and how we chose the model.** Four fields (agreement, emotional charge, hostility, personal attack); only the two texts in the prompt (no names, subreddits or labels). Pilot of 150 human-labelled edges, split dev/held-out before any prompt edit. Model chosen by pre-registered rule D-M1 (pilot accuracy, valid JSON, throughput), never by ground truth. Result: qwen3.5:9b, prompt v2: held-out accuracy 0.707, kappa 0.439, combined 0.72. State plainly: about 28% of signs are wrong.
- Visual: small table llama v1 0.587 -> v2 0.627 -> qwen 0.733 dev -> 0.707 held-out (from the guide).

**Slide 7: Pipeline A and Pipeline B.** A: additive rule table (disagree +1, charge 0 to 2, hostility 0/3/6, personal attack +1; clipped 0 to 10); P_A = weekly mean. B: edge sign w = (p_agree - p_disagree)(1 - p_neutral) -> weekly signed user graph (giant component) -> frustration of the best two-camp split (spectral + annealing heuristic) -> P_B = 1 - F_obs/F_null (sign-shuffle null). Sign-source controls: binary, VADER, unsigned.
- Say: "A asks how hostile; B asks whether the week splits into two camps."

**Slide 8: Does the tool work? Synthetic validation.** Graphs with known polarization: P_B rises with polarization (Spearman 0.964), recovers planted camps (balanced accuracy 0.51 / 0.81 / 0.97), handles unequal camps, heuristic optimal 10/10. 6 of 8 pre-stated checks pass; 2 fail and we report them (size flatness CV 0.46; separation level 0.172 < 0.2, though 26.8 null-sd below shuffle). The story: run 1 exposed under-converged annealing, fixed the budget (D-B3a), criteria unchanged.
- Key warning that matters for Reddit: P_B depends on mean degree. At our density (about 3) P_B is only 0.02 to 0.06 even at moderate polarization, so expect values near 0.

**Slide 9: Pre-registration and guardrails.** Eval spec v1 (tag eval-spec-v1) written BEFORE any comparison: Spearman levels and first differences, circular-shift permutation p, 4-week block bootstrap for delta rho, partial rho controlling for volume. Rule clarification D-E3 (also before 5% numbers). Nothing tuned against GT.
- Handoff: "With the tool validated and the rules fixed, we ran it on real data."

## Act 4. The experiments (slides 10 to 15)

**Slide 10: Tier 0 (1%, 230 edges/week).** 24,037 edges, 0 failures. B usable in 52 of 53 weeks (33 at 60/week). Result: A negative vs both truths (-0.43, -0.37), B about +0.11/+0.12 (n.s.), A vs B 0.11. No winner. This told us 1% is too weak and drove the move to 5%.

**Slide 11: Scaling to 5%: the speed wall and what we did.** Mac: 0.7 edges/s, about 245 h for 5%. New machine (RTX 3090): about 2.5 to 2.9 pairs/s, about 55 to 60 h. Finding: the server runs qwen3.5 on a single slot (4/8/16 parallel slots all 1.30 edges/s in the benchmark), so extra slots do not help. One crash (HTTP 500, 6-minute gap) recovered with zero loss from the cache. Run stopped at 22% to free the GPU. Thread-prefix ordering means the 22% is whole threads over all 53 weeks. (Figure not needed; a one-line timeline.)

**Slide 12: What the series look like.** `fig1_weekly_series.png`. The truths fall from spring to summer (and drop near 2016-06-27); A drifts upward in autumn; B is noisy around zero.

**Slide 13: The head-to-head.** `fig2_rho_by_pipeline.png` (rho and permutation p for A and B vs each truth) plus the delta table. Key numbers: A -0.44 / -0.38; B -0.05 / +0.07; delta on GT#2 +0.44 [0.06, 0.70].

**Slide 14: Is the gap stable?** `fig3_delta_over_checkpoints.png`: the delta point estimate is positive at every cut, but the CI included 0 at 30k, 53k, 77k and 102k and cleared it only at 136k. rho_B against GT#2 stays within 0.00 to +0.07 at every cut; only rho_A moves (from -0.19 to -0.38).

**Slide 15: Do the inferred camps match the real camps?** `fig4_camp_recovery.png`: LLM signs 0.531 vs null 0.524 (8/53 weeks above p95); VADER 0.534; unsigned 0.500. The LLM-signed camps are barely above chance and not better than free sentiment.

**Slide 16: The answer to RQ1 so far.** Three bullets:
1. Under our pre-registered rule, B passes narrowly on GT#2 (delta +0.44, CI [0.06, 0.70], rho_B +0.07).
2. But B itself is about zero (p 0.75) and the gap exists because A is anti-correlated with the truths; the sample-matched check shows no winner; no multiple-testing correction.
3. So: no evidence yet that the network tracks real polarization; no evidence that it does worse. Both weak at this sample size.
- Say it like this: "We are not claiming the network wins. We are claiming that, with a prediction we wrote down in advance, the network is the one that is not wrong, and that is a weak statement we want to strengthen."

## Act 5. What is left (slides 17 to 18)

**Slide 17: Limits (say them before someone else does).** 22% prefix, one model/prompt, 72% sign accuracy, A and B share a call, P_B insensitive at our density, camp labels provisional, composition and 06-27 not yet controlled, Holm/noise ceilings/baselines not done.

## Style notes for the deck
- Colour is fixed to identity everywhere: **Pipeline A = orange (#eb6834), Pipeline B = blue (#2a78d6), ground truths = neutral ink**. Keep that in every slide and every chart.
- Always put the coverage on slides that use 5% numbers: "22% of the 5% tier, 135.7k edges, 53 weeks".
- Never show a rho without its permutation p or CI.
- Use ranks and signs, not levels: P_B levels are tiny by construction at our density.
